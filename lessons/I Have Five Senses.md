## 🎮 Games (English intros · reusable for qkids picture-book lessons)

### Game 1 — Sense Detective  ·  [Warm-up, ~3 min]
**English intro:** A fast warm-up that wakes up the five-senses words before you open the book. The teacher gives a one-line clue built on *"I can [sense] something [adjective]"*, and Koko guesses the object or the sense. Then swap roles so Koko makes the clues — great for a shy start and for checking he knows the words.
**How to play:** Teacher: "I can taste something cold and sweet. What is it?" → Koko: "Ice cream!" Then Koko: "I can see something blue." → Teacher guesses.
**Sample language:** "I can [sense] something [adjective]. What is it?"

### Game 2 — Simon Says: Senses  ·  [Mid-lesson energy break, ~3 min]
**English intro:** A movement game that locks each body part to its sense. Koko follows the command and names the sense aloud. Use it when attention dips (around page 5–6). It directly builds the *eyes→see, nose→smell, ears→hear, mouth→taste, hands→feel* links from the last page, and works for ANY book that names body parts.
**How to play:** "Simon says touch your nose — what sense?" → "Smell!" / "Simon says cover your ears — what sense?" → "Hear!" Drop "Simon says" on purpose to trick him.
**Sample language:** "Simon says touch your [body part]. What sense?"

### Game 3 — Five Senses Bingo  ·  [Wrap-up, ~4 min]
**English intro:** A closing review on a tiny bingo board (see / hear / taste / smell / feel, each with one small picture). The teacher reads a sentence from the book; Koko covers the matching sense. First line wins a high-five. It checks recall of the whole book and can be reused with any vocabulary set by swapping the board.
**How to play:** Teacher: "I can hear the birds." → Koko covers *hear*. "I can feel the water." → covers *feel*. "I can smell the flowers." → covers *smell*.
**Sample language:** "I can [sense] the [object]."
